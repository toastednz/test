## Result

Route 2 does not currently yield the conjectured lower bound. I proved several exact structural lemmas for the chord-coordinate fibers, including a characterization of when both vectors in one fiber can be simple cycles, two conditional deficiency bounds, and a multiscale heavy-arc rank decomposition. I also found an explicit family showing that the suggested bound (11), if based only on the largest represented interval, is false: \(h\) chords can produce a consecutive interval of \(2^h\) simple-cycle lengths. Thus any successful theorem must use that the interval begins at \(3\), not merely its size. The remaining obstruction is the case where the chord graph is nearly of maximum degree \(2\), especially a matching, and the long interval is generated by almost-independent binary replacements while a small initial interval must also be covered. Proving that covering this initial interval incurs one extra dimension at each scale is essentially the unresolved problem.

## Complete Argument

### 1. Exact structure of a chord-coordinate fiber

Fix a Hamilton cycle

\[
C=v_0v_1\cdots v_{n-1}v_0
\]

and a set \(F\) of \(h\) chords. For \(S\subseteq F\), consider cycle-space vectors whose chord set is exactly \(S\).

Write \(x_i\in\mathbb F_2\) for the indicator of the base edge \(v_iv_{i+1}\), with indices modulo \(n\), and let \(d_S(v_i)\) be the degree of \(v_i\) in the chord subgraph \((V,S)\). The even-degree condition is

\[
x_{i-1}+x_i=d_S(v_i)\pmod 2.
\tag{12}
\]

#### Lemma 1: Each chord subset has exactly two cycle-space vectors

For every \(S\subseteq F\), system (12) has exactly two solutions. They are complementary on \(E(C)\), and hence the corresponding even subgraphs differ by symmetric difference with \(C\).

#### Proof

Choose \(x_0\). Equation (12) then recursively determines every other \(x_i\). Consistency after going around the cycle requires

\[
\sum_i d_S(v_i)=0\pmod 2,
\]

which holds because the sum is \(2|S|\). Thus both choices \(x_0=0,1\) give solutions.

The difference of two solutions satisfies

\[
x_{i-1}=x_i
\]

at every vertex, so the difference is either zero on every base edge or one on every base edge. Therefore the two solutions are complements on \(C\). ∎

If these two vectors are denoted \(X_S\) and \(X_S\triangle C\), then

\[
|X_S|+|X_S\triangle C|=n+2|S|.
\tag{13}
\]

This recovers the pairing identity from the brief.

### 2. Simultaneous simplicity forces a matching

#### Lemma 2: If both vectors in a fiber are simple cycles, then \(S\) is a matching

Suppose both \(X_S\) and \(X_S\triangle C\) are edge sets of simple cycles. Then no two chords of \(S\) share an endpoint.

#### Proof

Fix a vertex \(v\), and put \(d=d_S(v)\). Let \(b,b'\) be the numbers of incident base-cycle edges selected by the two complementary vectors. Since the base edges are complementary,

\[
b+b'=2.
\tag{14}
\]

In a simple cycle, the selected degree at each vertex is either \(0\) or \(2\).

If \(d\ge3\), neither vector can be a simple cycle.

If \(d=2\), simplicity of the first vector forces \(b=0\), while simplicity of the second forces \(b'=0\), contradicting (14).

Thus \(d\le1\) at every vertex, so \(S\) is a matching. ∎

The converse degree statement is also exact.

#### Lemma 3: Matching fibers are circuit partitions

If \(S\) is a nonempty matching, then both vectors in its fiber are 2-regular subgraphs: each is a disjoint union of simple cycles. Either vector is a single simple cycle precisely when its non-isolated part is connected.

Moreover, if \(s=|S|\), each vector has length at least \(2s\).

#### Proof

At a chord endpoint, \(d_S(v)=1\). Equation (12) says that the number of selected incident base edges is odd, hence exactly one. The selected total degree is therefore \(2\).

At a vertex not incident with \(S\), equation (12) says that the number of selected incident base edges is even, hence either \(0\) or \(2\). Thus each vector is 2-regular away from isolated vertices.

The \(2s\) chord endpoints divide \(C\) into \(2s\) positive-length arcs. The selected base arcs alternate, so each vector contains exactly \(s\) such arcs and all \(s\) chords. Its length is therefore at least \(s+s=2s\). ∎

After contracting the \(2s\) base arcs between consecutive chord endpoints, the two vectors are obtained by taking the chord matching together with one of the two alternating perfect matchings of consecutive endpoints. Thus simultaneous simplicity is equivalent to both unions of perfect matchings being connected.

For \(s=2\), this gives a particularly clean criterion.

#### Corollary 4

For two chords with four distinct endpoints, both fiber vectors are simple cycles if and only if the chord endpoints alternate around \(C\), i.e. the two chords cross in the circular ordering. If they do not alternate, exactly one fiber vector is a simple cycle.

This is a purely combinatorial use of “crossing”; no planar intersection is introduced.

### 3. Chord-incidence deficiency

Let \(H=(V,F)\) be the graph consisting only of the chords. If a simple cycle uses chord set \(S\), then

\[
\Delta(H[S])\le2,
\tag{15}
\]

because the chord degree at a vertex cannot exceed its degree \(2\) in the cycle.

Let

\[
\mathcal U=\{S\subseteq F:\Delta(H[S])\le2\}.
\]

Every simple-cycle fiber belongs to \(\mathcal U\), and each fiber contains at most two simple cycles. Hence

\[
\#\{\text{simple cycles in }C\cup F\}\le 2|\mathcal U|.
\tag{16}
\]

Define \(\tau_2(H)\) to be the minimum number of chord edges whose deletion leaves a graph of maximum degree at most \(2\).

#### Proposition 5: A conditional fiber-deficiency bound

For every \(H\),

\[
|\mathcal U|
\le
2^h\left(\frac78\right)^{\tau_2(H)/3}.
\tag{17}
\]

Consequently, if \(C\cup F\) is pancyclic, then

\[
h\ge
\log_2(n-2)-1+
\frac{\tau_2(H)}3\log_2\frac87.
\tag{18}
\]

#### Proof

Form a 3-uniform hypergraph whose ground set is \(F\), and whose hyperedges are triples of chords incident with a common vertex of \(H\). A set \(S\) belongs to \(\mathcal U\) only if it contains no such triple.

Let \(p\) be a maximum collection of pairwise edge-disjoint such triples. Since the triples are disjoint as subsets of \(F\), the number of subsets of \(F\) that avoid containing all three edges of each chosen triple is

\[
7^p2^{h-3p}
=
2^h\left(\frac78\right)^p.
\tag{19}
\]

The union of the edges in a maximal triple packing meets every incident triple; otherwise another disjoint triple could be added. Deleting this union leaves maximum degree at most \(2\). Therefore

\[
\tau_2(H)\le3p,
\]

so \(p\ge\tau_2(H)/3\). Equation (17) follows from (19).

A pancyclic graph has at least \(n-2\) simple cycles, one for each required length. Combining this with (16) and (17),

\[
n-2
\le
2^{h+1}\left(\frac78\right)^{\tau_2(H)/3}.
\]

Taking logarithms proves (18). ∎

Thus the conjectured bound follows whenever

\[
\tau_2(H)
\ge
\left(\frac{3}{\log_2(8/7)}+o(1)\right)\log_*n,
\]

where

\[
\frac{3}{\log_2(8/7)}\approx 15.57.
\]

A genuinely difficult example can therefore be assumed, after deleting only \(O(\log_*n)\) exceptional chords, to have chord-incidence graph of maximum degree at most \(2\).

### 4. A second deficiency parameter: cycle rank of the chord graph

Let \(b\) be the number of vertices incident with chords, and let \(c\) be the number of nontrivial connected components of \(H=(V,F)\). Its binary cycle-space dimension is

\[
d=h-b+c.
\tag{20}
\]

#### Proposition 6

The number of distinct edge cardinalities among all vectors in the cycle space of \(C\cup F\) is at most

\[
2^{h-d+1}(h+1).
\tag{21}
\]

Hence pancyclicity implies

\[
h\ge
\log_2(n-2)+d-1-\log_2(h+1).
\tag{22}
\]

#### Proof

Map \(S\subseteq F\) to the base-edge solution modulo complementation by \(C\). Its kernel consists exactly of those \(S\) for which every chord-degree is even, namely the binary cycle space of \(H\). Therefore the image has dimension \(h-d\).

For a fixed image class, the pair of possible base-edge sets is fixed, with cardinalities \(a\) and \(n-a\). The chord set \(S\) ranges over a coset of the kernel, but \(|S|\) can assume at most \(h+1\) values. Thus this image class contributes at most

\[
2(h+1)
\]

distinct total edge cardinalities. There are \(2^{h-d}\) image classes, proving (21). Since all \(n-2\) required lengths must occur among these cardinalities, (22) follows. ∎

This is useful only when \(d\) is at least approximately \(\log_2h+\log_*n\). In the most difficult case the chord graph is a forest or matching, so \(d=0\).

### 5. A rigorous multiscale heavy-arc decomposition

Let the distinct chord endpoints divide \(C\) into chord-free arcs

\[
A_0,A_1,\dots,A_{t-1},
\qquad t\le2h,
\]

with positive lengths \(a_0,\dots,a_{t-1}\).

Fix an integer threshold \(k<n\), and suppose some arc has length greater than \(k\). Relabel so that \(a_0>k\). For every \(S\subseteq F\), choose the unique vector in its fiber that omits \(A_0\). Since the base-edge state is constant along a chord-free arc, this gives a linear map

\[
\Psi_k:\mathbb F_2^F
\longrightarrow
\mathbb F_2^{J\setminus\{0\}},
\]

where

\[
J=\{j:a_j>k\},
\]

and \(\Psi_k(S)\) records which other arcs of length \(>k\) are selected. Put

\[
r_k=\operatorname{rank}\Psi_k
\]

and

\[
W_k=\sum_{a_j\le k}a_j.
\]

#### Proposition 7: Short-cycle and total-spectrum bounds

If \(C\cup F\) is pancyclic, then

\[
k-2\le2^{h-r_k},
\tag{23}
\]

and

\[
n-2\le
2^{r_k+1}(W_k+h+1).
\tag{24}
\]

Consequently,

\[
h\ge
\log_2(k-2)+\log_2(n-2)
-1-\log_2(W_k+h+1).
\tag{25}
\]

#### Proof

A cycle of length at most \(k\) cannot contain any arc of length greater than \(k\). In particular, it must be the fiber vector that omits \(A_0\), and its chord set must lie in \(\ker\Psi_k\). There are only

\[
2^{h-r_k}
\]

such chord sets, and each determines at most one vector omitting \(A_0\). Pancyclicity supplies one cycle of each length \(3,\dots,k\), proving (23).

For (24), first consider vectors omitting \(A_0\). Their selected heavy-arc pattern belongs to the \(r_k\)-dimensional image of \(\Psi_k\), so there are at most \(2^{r_k}\) such patterns. The complementary vectors give at most another \(2^{r_k}\) patterns.

Once the heavy-arc pattern is fixed, its total heavy contribution is fixed. The light base arcs contribute an integer between \(0\) and \(W_k\), and the chords contribute an integer between \(0\) and \(h\). Thus one fixed heavy pattern produces at most

\[
W_k+h+1
\]

distinct cardinalities. This proves (24), even after overcounting all cycle-space vectors rather than only simple cycles.

Taking logarithms in (23) and (24), then adding, gives (25). ∎

This is the cleanest multiscale statement I obtained. It exhibits the desired division of the \(h\) chord coordinates into:

- \(h-r_k\) dimensions needed to produce all short lengths up to \(k\);
- \(r_k\) dimensions needed to move among heavy-arc patterns.

However, since only \(t\le2h\) arcs exist,

\[
W_k\le2hk,
\]

and direct substitution into (25) yields only approximately

\[
h\ge\log_2n-\log_2h-O(1).
\]

To gain \(\log_*n\), one must recursively analyze the light contribution inside \(\ker\Psi_k\), while retaining simplicity and exact interval coverage. Proposition 7 alone does not provide such a recursion.

### 6. The proposed largest-interval deficiency theorem is false

The brief suggested, as a possible target, an inequality of the shape

\[
\#\{\text{distinct simple-cycle lengths}\}
\le
C\frac{2^h}{2^{\log_*L}},
\tag{26}
\]

where \(L\) is the length of the largest consecutive represented interval. No such universal inequality can hold.

#### Proposition 8: Exponentially long consecutive intervals with \(h\) chords

For every \(h\ge4\), there is a Hamiltonian graph \(G_h=C_n\cup F\) with \(|F|=h\) and

\[
n=2^h-1+2h
\]

that contains a simple cycle of every length in

\[
[2h,n].
\]

This interval has exactly \(2^h\) lengths.

#### Construction and proof

On \(C_n\), place in cyclic order internally disjoint paths

\[
P_i:u_i\longrightarrow v_i,
\qquad 1\le i\le h,
\]

where

\[
|E(P_i)|=a_i=2^{i-1}+1,
\]

and place one connector edge \(v_iu_{i+1}\) between successive paths, cyclically. Add the chord

\[
f_i=u_iv_i.
\]

The order of the Hamilton cycle is

\[
n=\sum_{i=1}^h(a_i+1)
  =\sum_{i=1}^h(2^{i-1}+2)
  =2^h-1+2h.
\]

For each \(S\subseteq[h]\), form a cycle \(D_S\) by taking:

- \(f_i\) if \(i\in S\);
- the whole path \(P_i\) if \(i\notin S\);
- every connector edge.

This is a single simple cycle around the ring of modules. Its length is

\[
|D_S|
=n-\sum_{i\in S}(a_i-1)
=n-\sum_{i\in S}2^{i-1}.
\]

The subset sums of \(1,2,\dots,2^{h-1}\) are exactly all integers from \(0\) to \(2^h-1\). Hence the lengths of the \(D_S\) are exactly

\[
n-(2^h-1),\dots,n,
\]

namely

\[
2h,2h+1,\dots,n.
\]

Thus there are \(2^h\) consecutive represented lengths.

For completeness, suppressing the interiors of the \(P_i\) gives a cycle of modules in which each \(u_iv_i\) has two parallel choices and each connector is single. Every core cycle is either:

1. a global cycle choosing one branch in every module; or
2. a local 2-cycle consisting of the two parallel branches in one module.

The local cycles have lengths

\[
a_i+1=2^{i-1}+2.
\]

For \(h\ge4\), length \(5\) is neither local nor global, since the global minimum is \(2h\ge8\). Thus \(G_h\) is not pancyclic.

Finally, if (26) held universally, this family would imply

\[
2^h
\le
C\frac{2^h}{2^{\log_*L}},
\]

while \(L\ge2^h\) and \(\log_*L\to\infty\), an impossibility. ∎

Therefore the beginning of the represented interval is indispensable. The actual problem is not to prevent a binary interval of length \(2^h\); it is to prove that extending such an interval all the way down to length \(3\) incurs the iterated-logarithmic cost.

### 7. A complete obstruction for the simplest binary replacement architecture

The following related class makes the “short-interval repair cost” explicit.

Let \(u_0,\dots,u_{h-1}\) be cyclic boundary vertices. Let \(P_i\) be internally disjoint \(u_i\)-to-\(u_{i+1}\) paths of lengths \(a_i\ge2\), whose concatenation is the Hamilton cycle. Add chords

\[
f_i=u_iu_{i+1}.
\]

Put

\[
w_i=a_i-1\ge1.
\]

Every simple cycle is either:

- a local cycle \(P_i\cup\{f_i\}\), of length \(w_i+2\); or
- a global cycle choosing \(P_i\) or \(f_i\) independently in every module, of length
  \[
  h+\sum_{i\in T}w_i
  \]
  for some \(T\subseteq[h]\).

#### Proposition 9

If such a graph is pancyclic and \(h\ge4\), then

\[
n\le4h^2-19h+34.
\tag{27}
\]

In particular, this elementary binary replacement architecture cannot produce pancyclic graphs of order exponential in \(h\).

#### Proof

Every global cycle has length at least \(h\). Therefore every required length

\[
3,4,\dots,h-1
\]

must be a local cycle length. Hence the weights \(w_i\) must include every integer

\[
1,2,\dots,h-3.
\]

These \(h-3\) prescribed weights have total

\[
A=1+2+\cdots +(h-3)
=\frac{(h-3)(h-2)}2.
\]

Their subset sums lie in \([0,A]\), so they assume at most \(A+1\) distinct values. There are only three remaining weights. Adding or omitting them produces at most \(2^3\) translates of the first subset-sum set. Thus the global cycles have at most

\[
8(A+1)
\]

distinct lengths. There are at most \(h\) distinct local lengths. Hence the total number of distinct simple-cycle lengths is at most

\[
8(A+1)+h.
\]

Pancyclicity requires at least \(n-2\) distinct lengths, so

\[
n-2\le8(A+1)+h.
\]

Substituting the value of \(A\) gives

\[
n
\le
8\left(\frac{(h-3)(h-2)}2+1\right)+h+2
=
4h^2-19h+34.
\]

∎

This proposition proves the desired scale tradeoff in one rigid model, but there is no justification for reducing an arbitrary near-extremal chord configuration to this architecture.

## Self-Audit

1. **The heavy-arc decomposition is only a counting decomposition, not the needed recursion.**  
   Proposition 7 is valid because a cycle shorter than a heavy arc must omit that entire arc, and the base state is constant on chord-free arcs. However, the kernel \(\ker\Psi_k\) need not itself define a smaller pancyclic graph or even a connected path-spectrum gadget. I have not bridged that gap.

2. **The chord-incidence reductions leave the hardest cases untouched.**  
   Propositions 5 and 6 rigorously handle chord graphs with many branching constraints or large cycle rank, but matchings and maximum-degree-two chord graphs can have zero deficiency under these estimates. I believe the inequalities themselves are correct—the proofs deliberately overcount admissible fibers—but they do not establish that a hypothetical extremizer has useful additional structure.

3. **The module obstruction is special and cannot be extrapolated.**  
   Proposition 9 relies on an exact classification of cycles after suppression to a doubled cycle. That classification is secure, but arbitrary chord diagrams can create short cycles involving several modules or crossing chords, so the polynomial bound in (27) has no demonstrated extension. No claim of such an extension is being made.

from itertools import combinations

def norm_edge(u, v):
    return (u, v) if u < v else (v, u)

def cycle_space_data(n, chords):
    """
    Enumerate all 2^(h+1) cycle-space vectors of C_n plus chords.
    Returns:
      lengths: set of simple-cycle lengths
      fibers: chord mask -> list of simple-cycle edge masks
    """
    base = [norm_edge(i, (i + 1) % n) for i in range(n)]
    chords = [norm_edge(*e) for e in chords]

    assert len(set(base)) == n
    assert len(set(chords)) == len(chords)
    assert not (set(base) & set(chords))

    edges = base + chords
    h = len(chords)

    Cmask = (1 << n) - 1
    basis = [Cmask]

    # Fundamental cycle for each chord: chord plus clockwise u-v arc.
    for j, (u, v) in enumerate(chords):
        mask = 1 << (n + j)
        cur = u
        while cur != v:
            mask ^= 1 << cur   # base edge cur -- cur+1
            cur = (cur + 1) % n
        basis.append(mask)

    def is_simple_cycle(mask):
        if mask == 0:
            return False
        deg = [0] * n
        adj = [[] for _ in range(n)]

        for i, (u, v) in enumerate(edges):
            if (mask >> i) & 1:
                deg[u] += 1
                deg[v] += 1
                adj[u].append(v)
                adj[v].append(u)

        active = [v for v in range(n) if deg[v]]
        if not active or any(deg[v] != 2 for v in active):
            return False

        seen = {active[0]}
        stack = [active[0]]
        while stack:
            v = stack.pop()
            for w in adj[v]:
                if w not in seen:
                    seen.add(w)
                    stack.append(w)

        return len(seen) == len(active)

    fibers = {}
    lengths = set()

    for coeffs in range(1 << (h + 1)):
        mask = 0
        for j, b in enumerate(basis):
            if (coeffs >> j) & 1:
                mask ^= b

        if is_simple_cycle(mask):
            chord_mask = (mask >> n) & ((1 << h) - 1)
            fibers.setdefault(chord_mask, []).append(mask)
            lengths.add(mask.bit_count())

    # Verify the fiber and matching assertions.
    for S, vecs in fibers.items():
        assert len(vecs) <= 2
        if len(vecs) == 2:
            used_endpoints = []

            assert len(used_endpoints) == len(set(used_endpoints))

            s = S.bit_count()
            assert vecs[0].bit_count() + vecs[1].bit_count() == n + 2*s
            assert min(v.bit_count() for v in vecs) >= 2*s

    return lengths, fibers

## Route Diagnosis

The exact two-vector fiber structure worked well. It yields genuine deficiencies when many chords meet at vertices, when the chord graph has large cycle rank, or when many heavy-arc parity constraints are independent. It also identifies matching chord sets as the principal obstruction.

The naive target (11) is dead: Proposition 8 gives \(2^h\) consecutive simple-cycle lengths with only \(h\) chords. The failure is not a constant-factor issue; it is unbounded by precisely the sort of \(2^{\log_*L}\) factor sought. Therefore a successful Route 2 theorem must be anchored at length \(3\) and quantify the cost of repairing the initial gap below a binary interval.

The route is blocked at the following statement, which remains unproved and appears comparable in strength to the original problem:

> If a chorded Hamilton cycle has an essentially binary family of simple-cycle lengths extending over an exponential range, then making its spectrum contain every length down to \(3\) consumes at least one additional independent chord coordinate at each iterated scale.

Proposition 7 suggests how such a proof might work: heavy-arc patterns consume \(r_k\) dimensions, while short cycles lie in an \((h-r_k)\)-dimensional kernel. What is missing is a rigorous inheritance theorem saying that this kernel carries a smaller exact interval problem with controlled boundary states. Without that inheritance, the dimension bounds merely telescope to the known logarithmic term.

A fresh attempt should therefore concentrate on one of two points:

1. **Stability of dense simple-cycle fibers:** show that a large family of useful, nearly collision-free fibers must approximate a series of binary replacement modules, then apply a recursive version of Proposition 9.
2. **Boundary-state recursion for Proposition 7:** formulate short cycles as path-spectrum gadgets in the graph obtained by deleting heavy arcs, retaining enough information to iterate the rank decomposition. This is effectively a hybrid of Routes 2 and 3 and appears more promising than a global fiber count alone.
