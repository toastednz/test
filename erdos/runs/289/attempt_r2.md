STATUS: BLOCKED

## Result

Route 2 yields a clean exact lattice formulation and several rigorous structural results, but not the required existence theorem. I prove: (i) an exact formula for the number of admissible \(k\)-run supports in \([2,N]\); (ii) the common-denominator weights of all blocks in a sufficiently long tail generate the full integer lattice, so there is no elementary gcd obstruction; (iii) nevertheless, subset sums cannot contain a macroscopic interval of any moderately fine lattice, by a sharp cardinality obstruction \(2^N=o(L_N)\); and (iv) maximal-valuation congruences exclude every selected denominator having a sufficiently large prime factor, quantitatively \(P^+(n)=O(N\log\log N/\log N)\) for solutions inside \([2,N]\). This last restriction is compatible with arbitrarily many separated dimers, so it does not settle the problem. I also isolate an exact near-solution of weight \(1+1/7140\) and a local “gap-slide” operation changing a valid support by exactly \(1/7140\), but I cannot make the two compatible. The route is blocked precisely at converting a primitive integer lattice generated using subtraction into a positive, compatible, zero-one subset sum equal to \(L_N\).

## Complete Argument

### 1. Common-denominator formulation

Fix \(N\ge 2\), and put
\[
L_N=\operatorname{lcm}(1,2,\dots,N),\qquad c_n=\frac{L_N}{n}.
\]
For \(S\subseteq[2,N]_{\mathbb N}\),
\[
\sum_{n\in S}\frac1n=1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sum_{n\in S}c_n=L_N.
\]
For a block \(B=[a,b]_{\mathbb N}\subseteq[2,N]\), define its integer weight
\[
C(B)=\sum_{n=a}^b c_n=L_Nw(a,b).
\]

Thus Route 2 asks for a compatible collection of exactly \(k\) blocks, each of length at least two, whose integer weights sum to \(L_N\).

### 2. Exact number of admissible supports

#### Lemma 2.1

The number of subsets \(S\subseteq[2,N]\) having exactly \(k\) maximal runs, every run of length at least two, is
\[
\boxed{\binom{N-k}{2k}},
\]
with the convention that the binomial coefficient is zero when \(N<3k\).

#### Proof

There are \(N-1\) available positions. Let the run lengths be
\[
\ell_1,\dots,\ell_k\ge2.
\]
Let \(g_1,\dots,g_{k-1}\ge1\) be the internal gap lengths, and let \(g_0,g_k\ge0\) be the initial and terminal gap lengths. Then
\[
\sum_{i=1}^k\ell_i+\sum_{i=0}^kg_i=N-1.
\]

Write
\[
\ell_i=2+\ell_i',\qquad
g_i=1+g_i'\quad(1\le i<k),
\]
where all primed variables and \(g_0,g_k\) are nonnegative. The resulting \(2k+1\) nonnegative variables have total
\[
(N-1)-2k-(k-1)=N-3k.
\]
By stars and bars, the number of solutions is
\[
\binom{(N-3k)+(2k+1)-1}{(2k+1)-1}
=\binom{N-k}{2k}.
\]
Every support has a unique such decomposition, proving the formula. ∎

In particular, any bounded construction for \(k\) runs needs
\[
N\ge3k.
\]

This also gives the exact information-theoretic bound
\[
\#\{\text{attainable sums from exactly \(k\) runs in }[2,N]\}
\le \binom{N-k}{2k}.
\]

### 3. The tail weights have no gcd obstruction

#### Lemma 3.1

Let \(D\) be a nonempty collection of divisors of an integer \(L\). Then
\[
\gcd_{d\in D}\frac{L}{d}
=
\frac{L}{\operatorname{lcm}(D)}.
\]

#### Proof

For every prime \(p\),
\[
v_p\left(\gcd_{d\in D}\frac Ld\right)
=\min_{d\in D}\bigl(v_p(L)-v_p(d)\bigr)
=v_p(L)-\max_{d\in D}v_p(d),
\]
which is exactly the \(p\)-adic valuation of
\[
L/\operatorname{lcm}(D).
\]
Equality follows prime by prime. ∎

#### Lemma 3.2

If \(m\le N/2\), then
\[
\operatorname{lcm}(m,m+1,\dots,N)=L_N.
\]

#### Proof

It suffices to show that every \(d\le N\) divides some member of \([m,N]\).

If \(d\ge m\), use \(d\) itself. If \(d<m\), put
\[
q=\left\lceil\frac md\right\rceil.
\]
Then \(qd\ge m\), while
\[
qd\le m+d-1\le2m-2\le N.
\]
Thus \(d\mid qd\) with \(qd\in[m,N]\). Hence every integer at most \(N\) divides the least common multiple of the tail. ∎

Combining the two lemmas gives
\[
\gcd_{m\le n\le N}c_n=1
\qquad(m\le N/2).
\]

More importantly, the same is true for the lattice generated by admissible block weights.

#### Lemma 3.3

Suppose \(N-m+1\ge3\). The additive subgroup of \(\mathbb Z\) generated by
\[
\left\{C([a,b]):m\le a<b\le N\right\}
\]
is the same as the subgroup generated by
\[
c_m,c_{m+1},\dots,c_N.
\]
Consequently, if \(m\le N/2\), the gcd of all block weights in the tail \([m,N]\) is \(1\).

#### Proof

Every block weight is a sum of the \(c_n\), so the block-weight subgroup is contained in the subgroup generated by the \(c_n\).

Conversely, for \(m\le j\le N-2\),
\[
c_j=C([j,j+2])-C([j+1,j+2]).
\]
Also,
\[
c_N=C([N-2,N])-C([N-2,N-1]),
\]
and then
\[
c_{N-1}=C([N-1,N])-c_N.
\]
Thus every \(c_n\) is an integer linear combination of block weights. The two generated subgroups are equal.

If \(m\le N/2\), Lemmas 3.1 and 3.2 show that the common gcd is \(1\). ∎

This eliminates one simple obstruction: the unrestricted block-weight lattice is primitive. However, the displayed combinations use subtraction and reuse overlapping blocks. They do not yield a positive compatible subset sum.

### 4. A cardinality obstruction to interval-coverage arguments

A suggested intermediate goal in Route 2 was to prove that block subset sums cover a long interval of multiples of a controlled modulus. There is a fundamental limitation to this approach.

#### Proposition 4.1

Let \(\eta>0\) be fixed. Suppose a family of subsets of \([2,N]\) has scaled sums containing every multiple of \(g_N\) in an integer interval of length at least \(\eta L_N\). Then
\[
g_N\ge
\frac{\eta L_N}{2^{N-1}+1}.
\]
In particular,
\[
g_N\ge \exp\bigl((1-\log2+o(1))N\bigr).
\]

The same conclusion holds a fortiori for run-structured subsets.

#### Proof

There are only \(2^{N-1}\) subsets of \([2,N]\), hence at most \(2^{N-1}\) distinct subset sums.

An interval of length at least \(\eta L_N\) contains at least
\[
\frac{\eta L_N}{g_N}-1
\]
multiples of \(g_N\). Therefore
\[
\frac{\eta L_N}{g_N}-1\le2^{N-1},
\]
which gives the first inequality.

Now
\[
\log L_N=\psi(N),
\]
where \(\psi\) is Chebyshev’s function. By the prime number theorem,
\[
\log L_N=N+o(N).
\]
Therefore
\[
\log\left(\frac{L_N}{2^{N-1}}\right)
=(1-\log2)N+o(N),
\]
proving the claimed exponential lower bound for \(g_N\). ∎

For exactly \(k\) runs, Lemma 2.1 sharpens the first bound to
\[
g_N\ge
\frac{\eta L_N}{\binom{N-k}{2k}+1}.
\]

Thus one cannot prove that admissible subset sums cover a normalized interval of length \(cL_N\) on the full integer lattice, or even on a lattice with fixed, polynomial, or subexponential modulus. Any successful Route 2 argument must target the special value \(L_N\) rather than establish broad fine-lattice coverage.

This does not prove that \(L_N\) itself is unattainable; it blocks only the most direct additive-basis implementation of Route 2.

### 5. Large-prime exclusion

The local congruence from the brief becomes particularly effective for primes having only a few multiples below \(N\).

#### Lemma 5.1

Let \(S\subseteq[2,N]\) satisfy
\[
\sum_{n\in S}\frac1n\in\mathbb Z.
\]
Let \(p\) be prime, and suppose \(p^2>N\). Put
\[
r=\left\lfloor\frac Np\right\rfloor,
\qquad
Q_r=\operatorname{lcm}(1,2,\dots,r),
\qquad
A_r=Q_rH_r=\sum_{u=1}^r\frac{Q_r}{u}.
\]
If
\[
p>A_r,
\]
then no selected denominator is divisible by \(p\).

#### Proof

Suppose some selected denominator is divisible by \(p\). Since \(p^2>N\), every such denominator has the form
\[
n=pu,\qquad 1\le u\le r,\qquad p\nmid u,
\]
and its \(p\)-adic valuation is exactly one.

Let
\[
U=\{u\in[1,r]:pu\in S\}.
\]
The maximal-valuation congruence gives
\[
\sum_{u\in U}u^{-1}\equiv0\pmod p.
\]
Since \(p>r\), \(Q_r\) is invertible modulo \(p\). Multiplying by \(Q_r\) yields
\[
\sum_{u\in U}\frac{Q_r}{u}\equiv0\pmod p.
\]
But if \(U\ne\varnothing\), this is a positive integer at most
\[
A_r<p.
\]
It therefore cannot be divisible by \(p\), a contradiction. Hence \(U=\varnothing\). ∎

#### Corollary 5.2

If \(p>N/3\) is prime and \(p>3\), then no selected denominator is divisible by \(p\).

#### Proof

Here \(r=\lfloor N/p\rfloor\le2\), and \(p^2>N\) because \(N<3p<p^2\). Also
\[
A_1=1,\qquad A_2=3.
\]
Lemma 5.1 applies. ∎

One can make this quantitative.

#### Corollary 5.3

There is an absolute \(N_0\) such that whenever \(N\ge N_0\) and
\[
S\subseteq[2,N],\qquad \sum_{n\in S}\frac1n\in\mathbb Z,
\]
every prime factor of every \(n\in S\) is at most
\[
O\left(\frac{N\log\log N}{\log N}\right).
\]

More explicitly, with
\[
r=\left\lfloor\frac{\log N}{4\log\log N}\right\rfloor,
\]
all prime factors are at most \(N/(r+1)\) for sufficiently large \(N\).

#### Proof

For large \(N\),
\[
Q_r\le r!,
\qquad
H_r\le1+\log r.
\]
Thus
\[
\log(Q_rH_r)
\le r\log r+\log(1+\log r)
\le \frac14\log N+o(\log N),
\]
so
\[
A_r=Q_rH_r<N^{1/3}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(N\).

Now let \(p>N/(r+1)\). Then
\[
s:=\left\lfloor\frac Np\right\rfloor\le r.
\]
Also \(p>N/(r+1)>\sqrt N\) for sufficiently large \(N\), so \(p^2>N\), and
\[
p>N^{1/2}>A_r\ge A_s.
\]
Lemma 5.1, applied with \(s\), shows that no selected denominator is divisible by \(p\). Hence all prime factors of selected denominators are at most \(N/(r+1)\), which has the stated order. ∎

This is a genuine global restriction on any bounded Route 2 solution. It is not, however, enough to control the number of runs.

#### Lemma 5.4

For every \(k\ge1\), there is a union of \(k\) separated dimers, all of whose prime factors are at most \(\sqrt M+1\), where \(M\) is its largest denominator.

#### Proof

Take
\[
S_k=\bigcup_{x=2}^{k+1}[x^2-1,x^2]_{\mathbb N}.
\]
Successive starts satisfy
\[
(x+1)^2-1=x^2+2x\ge x^2+4,
\]
so the dimers are separated by nonempty gaps.

The largest denominator is
\[
M=(k+1)^2.
\]
Every prime factor of \(x^2\) is at most \(x\), and every prime factor of
\[
x^2-1=(x-1)(x+1)
\]
is at most \(x+1\). Hence all prime factors occurring in \(S_k\) are at most
\[
k+2=\sqrt M+1.
\]
∎

These sets are not asserted to have integer reciprocal sum. They show that even the strong smoothness conclusion of Corollary 5.3 is geometrically compatible with arbitrarily many separated runs. A disproof would need simultaneous congruences across many primes, not merely exclusion of large prime factors.

### 6. An exact near-solution and a local correction mechanism

The Hickerson–Montgomery identity contains a useful target-\(1\) near miss.

#### Lemma 6.1

The four valid separated blocks
\[
[4,6],\quad[9,10],\quad[17,18],\quad[34,35]
\]
have reciprocal sum
\[
1+\frac1{7140}.
\]

#### Proof

Over the common denominator \(21420\), their numerators are respectively
\[
13209,\qquad4522,\qquad2450,\qquad1242.
\]
Their sum is
\[
13209+4522+2450+1242=21423
=21420+3.
\]
Since \(3/21420=1/7140\), the claim follows. All four blocks have length at least two and are mutually separated. ∎

There is a geometric way to change a support by exactly the same amount.

#### Lemma 6.2: gap slide

Let
\[
a\le n-2,\qquad b\ge n+3.
\]
Define
\[
U=[a,n]\cup[n+2,b],
\qquad
V=[a,n-1]\cup[n+1,b].
\]
Both \(U\) and \(V\) consist of exactly two separated runs, each of length at least two, and
\[
\sum_{m\in U}\frac1m-\sum_{m\in V}\frac1m
=\frac1n-\frac1{n+1}
=\frac1{n(n+1)}.
\]

#### Proof

The two supports differ only in that \(U\) contains \(n\) whereas \(V\) contains \(n+1\). The run-length and gap claims follow directly from the bounds on \(a,b\). The weight difference is therefore
\[
\frac1n-\frac1{n+1}=\frac1{n(n+1)}.
\]
∎

For \(n=84\), this difference is exactly
\[
\frac1{84\cdot85}=\frac1{7140}.
\]
For example,
\[
U=[82,84]\cup[86,87],\qquad
V=[82,83]\cup[85,87].
\]

Thus, if one could construct a representation of \(1+1/7140\) containing the \(U\)-configuration in an isolated location, sliding its gap would give a representation of \(1\) with the same number of runs. The known four-block near-solution does not contain this configuration, and simply adjoining it changes the total by its full positive weight. An exact replacement of some existing blocks by \(U\) would be needed. I have no proof that such a replacement exists.

### 7. Precise block

The strongest Route 2 facts above point in opposing directions:

1. The integer lattice generated by all tail block weights is primitive.
2. Fine interval coverage is information-theoretically impossible.
3. Prime congruences force smooth denominators, but smooth separated supports exist with arbitrary run count.
4. The primitive-lattice proof relies on subtraction and overlapping blocks:
   \[
   c_j=C([j,j+2])-C([j+1,j+2]).
   \]
   Neither operation is allowed in the desired positive zero-one subset sum.
5. No argument presently converts
   \[
   \gcd\{C(B)\}=1
   \]
   into a compatible block subset whose sum is the distinguished value \(L_N\), let alone one with every sufficiently large prescribed run count.

That conversion is essentially the key lemma requested by Route 2 and remains unproved. I therefore mark the route blocked rather than claiming that the lattice and congruence results solve the problem.

## Self-Audit

1. **The principal weakness is the missing positive-subset theorem.**  
   The proof only establishes that block weights generate \(\mathbb Z\) using arbitrary integer coefficients. It does not establish a zero-one representation, positivity, compatibility, or prescribed run count. I believe the stated lattice result because every individual \(c_n\) is explicitly written as an integer combination of block weights, but I explicitly make no stronger inference.

2. **The cardinality obstruction invokes the prime number theorem.**  
   The asymptotic \(\log L_N=N+o(N)\) is external. It is a standard theorem, and only the quantitative exponent uses it; the exact inequality
   \[
   g_N\ge\eta L_N/(2^{N-1}+1)
   \]
   is elementary and unconditional.

3. **The large-prime exclusion may appear stronger than it is.**  
   It depends on the chosen bound \(N\), and it controls individual prime factors rather than all simultaneous residue cancellations. The proof itself is valid because it examines every possible cofactor \(u\le\lfloor N/p\rfloor\). Lemma 5.4 deliberately demonstrates why the conclusion alone cannot bound the run count.

Exact dynamic programming for bounded \(N\), tracking run count and whether the current run has length \(0,1,\ge2\):

```python
def exact_run_dp(N, kmax, target_num=1, target_den=1, fixed=None):
    """
    Returns True iff some S subset [2,N] has the requested target,
    at most kmax runs during the DP, no singleton runs, and obeys
    optional fixed bits fixed[n] in {0,1}.

    State:
      0 = previous bit is 0
      1 = current selected run has length exactly 1
      2 = current selected run has length at least 2
    """
    if fixed is None:
        fixed = {}

    L = lcm_upto(N)
    if (L * target_num) % target_den:
        return False
    target = L * target_num // target_den

    # dp[(runs, state)] is the set of achievable integer weights.
    dp = {(0, 0): {0}}

    for n in range(2, N + 1):
        w = L // n
        allowed = (fixed[n],) if n in fixed else (0, 1)
        ndp = {}

        for (runs, state), sums in dp.items():
            for bit in allowed:
                if bit == 0:
                    # A run of length one may not terminate.
                    if state == 1:
                        continue
                    key = (runs, 0)
                    ndp.setdefault(key, set()).update(sums)
                else:
                    if state == 0:
                        if runs == kmax:
                            continue
                        key = (runs + 1, 1)
                    elif state == 1:
                        key = (runs, 2)
                    else:
                        key = (runs, 2)

                    out = ndp.setdefault(key, set())
                    for s in sums:
                        t = s + w
                        if t <= target:   # positivity makes this safe
                            out.add(t)

        dp = ndp

    witnesses = []
    for (runs, state), sums in dp.items():
        if state != 1 and target in sums:
            witnesses.append(runs)
    return sorted(set(witnesses))
```

```python
def run_data(mask, m):
    bits = [(mask >> i) & 1 for i in range(m)]
    lengths = []
    i = 0
    while i < m:
        if bits[i] == 0:
            i += 1
            continue
        j = i
        while j < m and bits[j] == 1:
            j += 1
        lengths.append(j - i)
        i = j
    return lengths

for N in range(2, 16):
    m = N - 1
    for k in range(0, N + 1):
        brute = 0
        for mask in range(1 << m):
            lengths = run_data(mask, m)
            if len(lengths) == k and all(x >= 2 for x in lengths):
                brute += 1
        formula = comb(N - k, 2 * k) if N - k >= 2 * k else 0
        assert brute == formula, (N, k, brute, formula)
```

## Route Diagnosis

**Proved ledger**

- Exact count:
  \[
  \#\{\text{\(k\)-run supports in }[2,N]\}=\binom{N-k}{2k}.
  \]
- The tail coefficients \(L_N/n\) have gcd \(1\) when the tail starts no later than \(N/2\).
- The integer lattice generated by all tail block weights is also \(\mathbb Z\).
- Macroscopic fine-lattice subset-sum coverage is impossible because \(2^N=o(L_N)\).
- Integer reciprocal sums inside \([2,N]\) cannot select denominators having prime factors larger than \(O(N\log\log N/\log N)\).
- Such smoothness is compatible with arbitrarily many separated dimers.
- There is a valid four-block sum \(1+1/7140\).
- A local gap slide can change a valid two-run configuration by exactly \(1/7140\) without changing its run count.

**Plausible but unproved**

- The special target \(L_N\) may be attainable despite the absence of broad interval coverage.
- A bounded movable-gap completion of the \(1+1/7140\) near-solution may exist.
- More sophisticated simultaneous \(p\)-adic constraints might reduce the candidate supports enough to enable a constructive argument, but no such theorem is presently established.

**Dead ends**

- “Primitive gcd implies interval coverage” fails: the gcd proof uses subtraction and overlapping blocks.
- Fine-lattice interval coverage is ruled out by cardinality unless the modulus is exponentially large.
- First-order large-prime exclusion cannot bound the run count; the explicit square dimers provide arbitrarily many smooth separated runs.
- The near-solution and gap slide have matching error \(1/7140\), but they cannot simply be adjoined because all weights are positive.

**Precise block**

Route 2 now requires a theorem selecting the special integer \(L_N\) from a very sparse family of positive compatible subset sums, with exact run-count control. Neither lattice primitivity, congruence pruning, nor generic additive-combinatorial coverage supplies that theorem. A fresh attempt should either:

1. search specifically for a movable-gap \(1+1/7140\) seed and then develop reusable count-changing gadgets; or
2. exploit simultaneous higher \(p\)-adic congruences rather than individual large-prime exclusions; or
3. identify a structured divisor-rich family for which the target \(L_N\), rather than a whole interval, has a canonical positive block partition.
