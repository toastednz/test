## Complete Argument

### 1. The splitting operation

Let \(x,u,v\in\mathbb N\) satisfy
\[
uv=x^2.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\frac1x=\frac1{x+u}+\frac1{x+v}.}
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed,
\[
(x+u)(x+v)
=x^2+x(u+v)+uv
=x(2x+u+v),
\]
while the numerator of the sum on the right is \(2x+u+v\).

This is equivalent to the formulation in the brief with \(t=u\) and \(v=x^2/t\).

#### Lemma 1: sibling leaves are never consecutive

If \(uv=x^2\), then \(x+u\) and \(x+v\) cannot be distinct consecutive integers.

**Proof.**
If \(|u-v|=1\), then \(\gcd(u,v)=1\). Since \(uv\) is a square, both \(u\) and \(v\) must be squares. Two positive squares cannot differ by \(1\), since
\[
r^2-s^2=(r-s)(r+s)=1
\]
has no solution with \(r,s\ge1\). ∎

Thus every legal adjacency created by splitting must couple leaves from different branches.

### 4. General parametrization of two-unit decompositions

The transformations above can be searched finitely.

#### Lemma 2

Let \(p,q\in\mathbb N\) be coprime. Positive integers \(a,b\) satisfy
\[
\frac1a+\frac1b=\frac pq
\tag{17}
\]
if and only if
\[
(pa-q)(pb-q)=q^2.
\tag{18}
\]

**Proof.**
Equation (17) is equivalent to
\[
pab=q(a+b).
\]
Thus
\[
p^2ab-pq(a+b)+q^2=q^2,
\]
which factors as (18). Conversely, expanding (18) recovers \(pab=q(a+b)\). Since both unit fractions are strictly smaller than their positive sum \(p/q\), one has \(pa-q>0\) and \(pb-q>0\). ∎

Consequently, all two-unit decompositions are parametrized by divisors \(D\mid q^2\) satisfying
\[
D\equiv -q\pmod p,
\]
through
\[
a=\frac{D+q}{p},\qquad
b=\frac{q^2/D+q}{p}.
\tag{19}
\]

For example, applying this to \(15/2408\) produces the pair \((301,344)\), while applying it to \(44/103845\) produces \((4515,4945)\).

There is no \(n\in\mathbb N\) such that

d(n)=\frac1{4515}+\frac1{4945}

**Proof.**

X(X+44)=q^2.

(X+22)^2=q^2+22^2,

and hence

### 6. Why a direct synchronized split of \(4515,4945\) fails

For roots \(x<y\), choose divisors
\[
u\mid x^2,\qquad v\mid y^2.
\]
The selected children \(x+u\) and \(y+v\) are adjacent precisely when
\[
u-v=(y-x)-1
\quad\text{or}\quad
u-v=(y-x)+1.
\tag{22}
\]

For
\[
x=4515,\qquad y=4945,
\]
the gap is \(430\), so (22) would require
\[
u-v\in\{429,431\}.
\tag{23}
\]
Both roots are odd. Every divisor of either square is therefore odd, so \(u-v\) is even. This contradicts (23).

Thus:

\[
\boxed{\text{No one-split child of \(4515\) can be adjacent to a one-split child of \(4945\).}}
\tag{24}
\]

If \(4945\) is retained and only \(4515\) is split, a child adjacent to it would have to be \(4944\) or \(4946\), requiring offsets \(429\) or \(431\). Neither divides
\[
4515^2,\qquad 4515=3\cdot5\cdot7\cdot43.
\]
If \(4515\) is retained and \(4945\) is split, every child of \(4945\) exceeds \(4945\), so it cannot be adjacent to \(4515\).

Hence the remaining two singleton defects cannot be closed by splitting neither, one, or both exactly once.

### 7. A general shallow-depth obstruction

#### Lemma 4

Let \(x<y\) be two nonadjacent singleton denominators.

1. Splitting exactly one of them once cannot produce a terminal legal support.
2. Splitting each exactly once cannot produce a terminal legal support.

**Proof.**

For part 1, suppose \(x\) is split and \(y\) retained. The terminal support has three terms. If all runs have length at least two, the three distinct denominators must form one run of length three. By Lemma 1, the two children of \(x\) are not consecutive, so they must be \(y-1\) and \(y+1\). Writing \(h=y-x\), the two split offsets are \(h-1\) and \(h+1\), whose product must be \(x^2\). Thus
\[
(h-1)(h+1)=x^2,
\]
or
\[
(h-x)(h+x)=1,
\]
which has no solution with \(x,h\in\mathbb N\).

If instead \(y\) is split and \(x\) retained, both children exceed \(y\), so the three terms cannot be consecutive because \(x<y\).

For part 2, suppose the four terminal denominators are distinct and every run has length at least two. They can then be partitioned into two pairs of consecutive denominators: either they are two dimers, or they form one run of length four and we pair its first two and last two terms.

By Lemma 1, each such pair must contain one child of \(x\) and one child of \(y\). Write the offsets of the children of \(x\) as \(u_1,u_2\) and those of \(y\) as \(v_1,v_2\), paired accordingly. Consecutiveness implies
\[
u_i-v_i\in\{(y-x)-1,(y-x)+1\}.
\]
Thus \(u_i\ge v_i\) for both \(i\), and consequently
\[
x^2=u_1u_2\ge v_1v_2=y^2,
\]
contrary to \(x<y\). Cross-branch duplicate denominators are also invalid in a terminal set because they would carry multiplicity two. ∎

This proves that any forward split-tree closure of the residual pair requires depth at least two on some branch.

    f = {}

        f[2] = f.get(2, 0) + 1

def split_options(x):
    x2 = x*x
    out = []
    for u in square_divisors(x):
        v = x2 // u
        if u <= v:
            out.append((x + u, x + v))
    return out

def canon(counter):
    return tuple(sorted((n,m) for n,m in counter.items() if m))

def run_lengths(support):
    xs = sorted(support)
    if not xs:
        return []
    ans = []
    cur = 1
    for i in range(1, len(xs)):
        if xs[i] == xs[i-1] + 1:
            cur += 1
        else:
            ans.append(cur)
            cur = 1
    ans.append(cur)
    return ans

def terminal(state):
    c = Counter(dict(state))
    if any(m != 1 for m in c.values()):
        return False
    return all(L >= 2 for L in run_lengths(c))

def defect_score(state):
    c = Counter(dict(state))
    duplicates = sum(max(0, m-1) for m in c.values())
    singletons = sum(L == 1 for L in run_lengths(c))
    return duplicates + singletons

def bounded_split_search(start, max_den, max_terms, state_limit=1_000_000):
    start = canon(Counter(start))
    heap = [(defect_score(start), sum(dict(start).values()), start)]
    seen = {start}

    while heap and len(seen) <= state_limit:
        _, terms, state = heappop(heap)
        if terminal(state):
            return state

        c = Counter(dict(state))
        for x in list(c):
            base = c.copy()
            base[x] -= 1
            if base[x] == 0:
                del base[x]

            for y, z in split_options(x):
                if max(y,z) > max_den or terms + 1 > max_terms:
                    continue
                nxt = base.copy()
                nxt[y] += 1
                nxt[z] += 1
                key = canon(nxt)
                if key in seen:
                    continue
                seen.add(key)
                heappush(heap, (defect_score(key), terms + 1, key))
    return None

## Route Diagnosis

**Proved ledger.**

- The split operation is equivalently parametrized by factor pairs \(uv=x^2\).
- Sibling leaves can never be consecutive.
- The Hickerson–Montgomery defective identity can be refined exactly to five legal runs plus the defects \(168,3612\).
- The actually occurring packet \(1/168+1/3612\) can be replaced compatibly by a synchronized packet that contributes the new legal dimer \([344,345]\) and leaves defects \(4515,4945\).
- All two-unit decompositions of a rational \(p/q\) admit the finite divisor parametrization \((pa-q)(pb-q)=q^2\).
- The residual \(1/4515+1/4945\) is not one dimer.
- The pair \(4515,4945\) cannot create an adjacent cross-branch pair after one split each; more generally, two nonadjacent singleton roots cannot be legally closed if each is split at most once.
- A natural common-child depth-two continuation gains \([5160,5161]\) but increases the singleton count from two to three.

**Plausible but unproved.**

- Some other two-unit parametrization of \(44/103845\), followed by a depth-two synchronized split, may reduce the defect count.
- A three-defect packet such as
  \[
  \left\{36120,118680,26630760\right\}
  \]
  may possess a finite closure unavailable to the two-defect packet.
- A successful terminal state may contain a reusable marked packet furnishing run-count increments with gcd \(1\).

**Dead ends diagnosed.**

- Direct synchronization of \(168,3612\) is arithmetically impossible; the intermediate two-unit rewrite was necessary.
- Direct synchronization of \(4515,4945\) is ruled out by parity.
- Resolving their common child \(5160\) by the \(t=1\) split creates a legal dimer but worsens the defect count.
- Iterating such “one dimer gained, more singleton defects emitted” steps without a finite closure only produces another infinite expansion and does not solve the problem.

**Precise block.**

The route now requires a finite exact closure of the residual packet, or a self-propagating packet macro with proved finite closures and count increments covering all sufficiently large integers. Neither has been established. This is not a routine completion: it retains the original problem’s positive, zero-one, and terminal-geometry difficulty. Even finding one closed support would still leave the eventual-\(k\) quantifier unless the closure exposes a repeatable count pump.

A fresh attempt should exhaust all two-unit decompositions of \(44/103845\), test every direct and depth-two synchronization exactly, and then enlarge the state space to three marked defects generated by common-child collisions. Any terminal support found should be mined for a recursive marked packet rather than treated merely as an isolated representation.
