# Erdős Problem #906 — candidate solution (affirmative)

**Problem** ([erdosproblems.com/906](https://www.erdosproblems.com/906)).
Is there an entire non-zero function $f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}$ such that, for
any infinite sequence $n_1<n_2<\cdots$, the set
$\{z : f^{(n_k)}(z)=0 \text{ for some } k\ge 1\}$ is everywhere dense?

**Context.** Erdős [Er82e] wrote that this was solved in the affirmative
"more than ten years ago" but gave no reference, and the erdosproblems.com
database lists it as OPEN, noting the natural attribution (Barth–Schneider)
does not contain the result. Tang's remark on the problem page: the question
is trivial for polynomials ($f^{(n)}\equiv 0$ eventually), so the intended
reading requires $f$ transcendental. Both proofs below produce transcendental
$f$.

## Reduction

It suffices to construct a transcendental entire $f$ with the stronger
property

> $(P'')$: for every nonempty open disc $D$ there is $N_D$ such that for all
> $n \ge N_D$, $f^{(n)}$ has a zero in $D$.

Indeed, given $(P'')$ and any infinite sequence $(n_k)$: every disc $D$
contains a zero of $f^{(n_k)}$ whenever $n_k \ge N_D$, so the union of the
zero sets over $k$ meets every disc. (Conversely note $(P'')$ is exactly the
statement that for every disc, all but finitely many derivatives vanish
somewhere in it — the natural cofinite form of the problem.)

## Proof 1 (Gaussian Entire Function; hole probabilities via harmonic rigidity)

Let $(\xi_m)_{m\ge0}$ be i.i.d. standard complex Gaussians and
$$f(z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\xi_m\,\frac{z^m}{\sqrt{m!}}$$
the planar Gaussian Entire Function. Almost surely $f$ is entire (coefficient
tail bound + Borel–Cantelli) and transcendental (all coefficients nonzero
a.s.).

Normalize $G_n=f^{(n)}/\sqrt{n!}=\sum_r \xi_{n+r} b_{n,r} z^r$ with
$b_{n,r}=\sqrt{(n+r)!/n!}\,/r!$. Then $G_n(z)$ is a centered complex Gaussian
with variance
$$S_n(|z|)=\sum_{r\ge0}\frac{(n+r)!}{n!\,(r!)^2}|z|^{2r}
 = e^{|z|^2}L_n(-|z|^2)$$
(Vandermonde), where $L_n$ is the Laguerre polynomial. Explicit two-sided
bounds give, for each fixed $r\ge0$,
$$\frac{\log S_n(r)}{2\sqrt n}\longrightarrow r ,$$
with the upper bound $L_n(-x)\le e^{2\sqrt{nx}}$ and the lower bound from the
single term $j=\lfloor r\sqrt n\rfloor$ plus Stirling. [Numerically confirmed:
ratios $0.4942,\,0.9942,\,1.9961$ at $n=10^5$ for $r=\tfrac12,1,2$.]

Fix a disc $D$, choose $\overline{B(c,2s)}\subset D$, and let
$A=\mathrm{avg}_{t}\,|c+se^{it}|$. Strict convexity of the circle gives
$\delta:=A-|c|>0$; set $\eta=\delta/8$. If $G_n$ were zero-free on $D$, then
$u_n=\frac1{\sqrt n}\log|G_n|$ is harmonic on $B(c,2s)$, and on the event
that (i) $\sup_{|z|\le R}|G_n|\le e^{M\sqrt n}$ (probability
$\ge 1-Ce^{-\sqrt n}$ by an $L^1$–Cauchy–Schwarz sup-bound and Markov),
(ii) $|u_n(c)-|c||<\eta$, and (iii) $|u_n(z_j)-|z_j||<\eta$ at $J=J(D)$
equally spaced points of $\partial B(c,s)$ — each of (ii),(iii) failing with
probability $\le 2e^{-\eta\sqrt n}$ by exact complex-Gaussian tails
$\Pr(|\zeta|^2\le e^{-2t})\le e^{-2t}$, $\Pr(\log|\zeta|\ge t)=e^{-e^{2t}}$ —
one derives a contradiction:
Harnack's inequality applied to $M-u_n\ge0$ on $B(c,3s/2)$ (constant $7$)
plus interior gradient estimates make $t\mapsto u_n(c+se^{it})$ Lipschitz
with an $n$-independent constant, so the $J$-point Riemann sums approximate
the circle average of $u_n$ within $\eta$; the mean value property then
forces $|u_n(c)-A|<3\eta$, hence $\delta\le|A-u_n(c)|+|u_n(c)-|c||<4\eta=
\delta/2$, absurd. Therefore
$$\Pr(G_n \text{ zero-free in } D)\;\le\; Ce^{-\sqrt n}+2(J+1)e^{-\eta\sqrt n},$$
summable in $n$. Borel–Cantelli over a countable basis of rational discs
yields: almost surely, for every disc $D$ there is $N_D$ with
$Z(f^{(n)})\cap D\ne\varnothing$ for all $n\ge N_D$. Any realization in the
intersection with $\{f\ \text{transcendental entire}\}$ satisfies $(P'')$.
$\blacksquare$

## Proof 2 (independent; first/second moment on smooth zero statistics)

Take instead $f(z)=\sum_m \xi_m z^m/(m!)^{1/4}$ (order-4 Gaussian Taylor
series). For any nondegenerate Gaussian analytic function $G$ and
$\phi\in C_c^\infty$, with $X_\phi=\sum_{G(z)=0}\phi(z)$:
$$\mathbb E X_\phi=\frac1{4\pi}\int\phi\,\Delta_z\log K(z,z)\,dA,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}X_\phi\le\frac1{96}\Big(\int|\Delta\phi|\Big)^2 ,$$
the variance bound following from $Y(z)=\log|G(z)|-\frac12\log K(z,z)-c_0$
having constant variance $\pi^2/24$ and Cauchy–Schwarz — crucially,
independent of $n$ and of the kernel. For this $f$, the first intensity of
$f^{(n)}$ on any fixed disc grows like $n^{3/4}$ (coefficient-index
concentration of width $n^{3/4}$), so choosing $\phi$ supported in $D$,
$0\le\phi\le1$, $\phi=1$ on a subdisc:
$$\Pr\big(f^{(n)}\text{ zero-free in }D\big)
\le \Pr\big(X_\phi=0\big)
\le \frac{\operatorname{Var}X_\phi}{(\mathbb E X_\phi)^2}
= O_D\!\big(n^{-3/2}\big),$$
summable; Borel–Cantelli and the countable basis argument finish as before.
$\blacksquare$
